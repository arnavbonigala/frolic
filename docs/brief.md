  Frolic

  Your friend group's plans, actually happening.

  In college, everyone wants to hang out, but nobody wants to be the one who always reaches out and gets "maybe" back. Plans die in the group chat, and people go weeks surrounded by friends without really seeing them. Side quest apps don't help, since they're built for one person with a "share with friends" button added at the end. Frolic sends your group a personalized in-person activity every day, based on who's free, and it unlocks once two of you tap "I'm down." It learns what your group likes from photos of past hangouts, and every quest you finish goes into a shared scrapbook.

  The pain we own
  Nobody wants to be the one who reaches out

  Every other complaint in the research traces back to this one. Dead plans, flaky friends and the burnt-out planner all start with someone deciding the ask isn't worth the risk of a "maybe." Frolic takes the ask away. The app proposes the plan, every answer stays private, and you only find out who's in once it's already happening.

  The core feature
  See who's free, commit privately, match on who you want there

  Whether you go usually depends on who else is going, so Frolic splits the low-risk part from the risky part. Who's free is visible: you see that Maya, Dev and Jordan are free after 5 before you decide anything. Who's down stays private. You tap "I'm down," either with anyone or with a condition like "only if Maya's in."

  Frolic looks for a set of two or more people whose conditions all hold, then unlocks for exactly them at the same moment. Nobody ever learns you said "only if Maya," nobody sees a pass, and if no match forms by the deadline the activity quietly disappears. Once it's on, the rest of the free friends see it with names ("It's on: Maya and Dev at Indian Rock, hop in?") and can join with zero risk, because it's already happening.

  This is the one thing Frolic has to be excellent at. The metric that matters is the share of activities that unlock and actually happen.

  What makes it exceptional
  Frolic says the awkward thing so you don't have to

  The private "I'm down" takes away the awkward ask. The same idea runs through every other awkward moment in a friend group: "I'm broke," "I'm tired," "only if she's going," "I can't stay late." Each one becomes a private setting, and the plan quietly fits everyone.

    1The reveal is choreographed
Convex schedules the reveal for one server time, so every committed phone flips at the same instant with a haptic, a little burst of confetti and the faces of who's coming. Then "leave in 8 min," live on-my-way bubbles after the match, auto check-in on arrival, and a quiet refill from the hop-in pool if someone drops.

    2Private constraints
Everyone secretly sets a budget cap, an energy level (chill or down for anything), a max distance and how late they can stay. The engine picks a plan inside everyone's limits, and nobody has to say "I can't afford that" out loud. Straight from the data: "only applicable if you have money" (7.2K likes).

    3"I'm down" from the lock screen
The daily drop is a notification with I'm down, Only if, and Not today buttons, so answering takes three seconds without opening the app. Once it's on, a Live Activity counts down "leave in 8" on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island.

    4Commit to a window, not a time
Tap "down 5 to 8pm." The matcher combines windows and "only if" conditions to find the time and group that gets the most people out: "6:30 works for 4 of you." Real constraint solving under one button.

    5A plan you can act on in ten seconds
A real place that's open now, the cost, walking or BART time for each person, a fair meeting point so nobody is more than about 12 minutes away, the actual sunset time, and a "why this" line from the group's taste.

    6Memories built to be shared
Check-in photos become a polaroid page automatically. A Wrapped-style group taste card ("Sunset Crew: golden-hour chasers, under $10, night owls") and a weekly recap sized for Stories. One in ten comments in the data is a friend tag, so this crowd shares by default.

  Skip on purpose: chat, comments, likes, followers and any feed. Each one dilutes "one decision a day."

  UX rules

    One decision a day. A single card and a single button. No feed and no browsing. From notification to "I'm down" should take under ten seconds.

    Nothing ever reads as rejection. There's no "declined" state, no read receipts and no list of who passed. Ignoring the card is the same as saying no.

    The reveal is the moment. Every committed phone flips together, with a haptic and the names of who's coming. That's what people tell their friends about.

    Plans are concrete. Always a real place, a time, a cost and walking minutes. "Go get food" never ships.

    Free is public, down is private. Show who's free so people can decide based on who's going. Keep every commitment, and every "only if," hidden until a match.

    Once it's on, show everything. After the unlock, names are visible to the rest of the free friends with a one-tap "Hop in." Joining something already happening carries no risk.

    Join from a link, never a contact wall. Howbout and Locket lose people by forcing invites before the app works. Frolic works with two people.

    Location stays approximate until a match. Friends see "12 min away," never a pin, until they're actually meeting.

    Never punish a missed day. The scrapbook counts nights out instead of a fragile streak. Lost streaks write the angriest reviews in this space.

    One drop a day, and that's it. Frolic sends the daily drop plus alerts only about things that involve you: "It's on" and "Hop in." No guilt nudges, no streak nagging. BeReal's "five a day and I can't turn it off" reviews are the warning.

    Sign in with Apple. No phone-number codes. Partiful and Bumble BFF reviews are full of codes that never arrive.

    The core loop is free forever. Paid extras are per group, never per person (printed scrapbooks, premium packs, bigger groups), with one-tap cancel and no trial that silently bills. Surprise charges drove 138 Timeleft and 92 Howbout complaints.

    Sober, daytime and cheap by default. Drinking dinners are opt-in. 222 and Timeleft reviewers kept asking for anything that isn't a bar.

  Look and feel
  A meadow that grows out of your friend group

  To frolic is to run through a field for no reason. The app should feel like that: sunlit, colorful, a little silly, and completely safe. Every visual choice serves one job, which is making it easy to say yes to seeing people.

  Who opens Frolic, and in what state

  A college student at 3pm, between classes, phone in hand, slightly bored or slightly lonely. They want to see people. What stops them is the ask: texting "anyone free?" and getting silence or a "maybe," or being the one who cares more. They arrive guarded. The screen has three seconds to lower that guard.

  The metaphor carries the mechanics

  The flower system is how the product explains itself without a tutorial. Free is a bloom. Down is a bud. A match is a bloom together. Hop in is room in the bunch. A memory is a flower in the garden. Learn one and the rest follow.

    Color means people
Each friend gets one flower color (pink, cobalt, moss, marigold, tomato, lilac) and it follows them everywhere: their flower, their ring, their dot on the way there. The ground is cream and sky so the colors sing. Chrome stays quiet.

    Type
SF Pro Rounded, heavy, for the big words: friendly, native, and crisp at every size. SF Pro for everything else. The custom wordmark is the only lettering we draw.

    Shape
Round everything. Petals, capsules, circles. Nothing sharp, nothing boxed in hard lines.

    Motion is wind
Things sway, bloom, drift and settle on springs. Nothing blinks, nags or counts down at you. The one burst of energy is the bloom at "It's on."

  Built with
  Built to win every best-use track

  Each sponsor gets one job that only it can do, and one moment in the demo where judges can see it working.

    CConvex: the database keeps the secret
Taps live in a table that no client query can read. Only the matcher touches it, so "who's down" can't leak even from a modded app. Around that: the Presence component runs live free status, the Workflow component runs every frolic as a durable job (drop, taps, match, reveal, leave in 8, check-in, expire or refill), a scheduled function fires the reveal at one server timestamp so every phone flips in the same frame, the Geospatial component finds open places and a fair meeting point, crons generate the golden-hour drop and the Thursday weekend drop, and live queries carry every change to every open phone instantly. The Rate Limiter component turns "one drop a day" from a promise into code. Demo moment: the Convex dashboard on screen shows taps landing while every phone still shows nothing.

    RRevyl: cloud phones play the rest of the friend group
Three real phones on stage plus five Revyl cloud devices, each one a friend driven by a plain-English script ("tap I'm down, only if Maya"), each placed somewhere in Berkeley with set_location. The judge watches an eight-person group match live, and the same scripts are our test suite: two taps in the same 100 ms must reveal once on both phones, a friend who backs out triggers a refill, the "only if" never leaks. Demo moment: the cloud phones stream on screen and flip to "It's on" together with ours.

    CCapy: agents that prove their own work
Captain plans the build, then parallel Build agents each take a lane (matcher, reveal, push, scrapbook, taste). Every agent runs Revyl through its CLI before opening a PR, so each PR arrives with a replay video of the feature working on a real device. We review and merge; the agents write and verify. Demo moment: one slide with the PR count and a wall of Revyl replays. Stretch: a judge asks for a tweak during Q&A, Capy ships it and Revyl verifies it before we leave the stage.

    SSupermemory: a group that remembers itself
Each person and each group get their own container. Personal memories hold private things (budget, "no car," "vegetarian") and never reach the group container. The group's profile holds shared taste, and the memory graph tracks how it changes: three weeks of passing on boba and Frolic stops suggesting it. Every pick gets a "why this" line pulled from real memory: "Because the Marina sunset in October was your best night." Demo moment: the taste card and the "why this" line, both quoting things the group actually did.

    WWhop: nobody fronts the money
When a plan costs something (kayak rental, show tickets, a pizza order), "It's on" comes with a one-tap Chip in through WhopCheckout and Apple Pay, so nobody pays for everyone and chases Venmo later. Whop's iOS checkout covers real-world goods and services at 2.7% + 30¢, and paying upfront is the strongest anti-flake there is. Later, local spots sell Frolic experiences through Whop, which is how the core stays free. Digital extras still go through StoreKit, as Apple requires.

  How it works
  Every feature, and the machinery behind it

  One app on SwiftUI for iOS 26, one Convex backend, and the newest on-device Apple frameworks doing the private work on the phone. Three facts shape the design: no $99 Apple account (so no remote push and no Sign in with Apple), everything has to run by 6pm, and every feature has to be something a judge can watch work.

  Two taps on the surface, a lot underneath

  The user sees one plan and one button. Everything below happens without them setting anything.

    What the user never has to doWhat Frolic does instead

      Say when they're freeReads busy times from the calendar on the phone (EventKit) and shares only "free till 8." Class schedules repeat, so Monday's guess is right by the second week.

      Pick a placeScores real candidates on weather, sunset, opening hours, price against everyone's private limits, and the group's taste. Then picks the spot that keeps the longest trip shortest, using each phone's own MapKit ETA.

      Pick a timeThe matcher tries every start time across everyone's windows and picks the one that gets the most people out.

      Check the weatherTwo hours before, a scheduled Convex check looks at the forecast again. If rain moves in, the plan swaps to the indoor backup and everyone's taps carry over.

      Tell it what they likeEvery yes, every ignore and every check-in photo (labeled on the phone) updates the group's Supermemory profile. Ignore boba three times and it stops coming back.

      Keep it fairRotates whose taste leads the pick, so one loud friend's favorites don't win every day.

      Rally a quiet groupWhen a group goes a week without a frolic, the next drop is their best-rated night again: "Indian Rock was your favorite. Same time?"

      Fill a dropped spotIf someone bails, the Workflow re-runs the match with the hop-in pool before anyone notices a gap.

      Check in, save photos, make the recapThe geofence checks you in, the photo goes straight to the scrapbook, and the taste card rewrites itself each week.

      Time the daily planLearns when each group actually answers and sends the drop then, anchored to the day's sunset.

  The features, one by one

    1Join a group from a link
The invite link opens the app with frolic://join/CODE. You pick your name and photo and you're in. With no paid account there's no Sign in with Apple, so the device id is the identity for the demo. The app works with two people. Build: joinGroup mutation, one onboarding screen.

    2Teach it your taste, privately
Pick five photos of past hangouts. On the phone, Vision labels each photo ("sunset," "pizza," "board game," "beach") and Foundation Models turns those labels into a typed Taste struct: activities, vibe, price feel. The photos never leave the phone. Only the tags go to the person's Supermemory container, and they merge into the group's container. Demo line: "Your photos never left your phone. Frolic just learned you're golden-hour people."

    3Free now, or free 3 to 6
One toggle and a window picker write to availability. The Presence component shows who has the app open right now. Each phone works out its own walking or transit minutes to today's spot with MapKit and uploads only the number, so friends see "12 min away," never a pin. Build: setFree mutation, Presence heartbeat, MapKit ETA.

    4The daily drop, generated for whoever's free
A Convex cron runs generateDrop for each group. It gathers real candidates: Berkeley places open during the overlap of people's windows, inside everyone's private limits (budget, distance, energy, home by), fitted to Open-Meteo's weather and sunset time. It asks Supermemory for the group's taste and what they've skipped lately. Then it hands Claude only those candidates and gets back a structured pick: place id, title, the line ("Clear and 64°. Golden hour starts at 4:28"), and a "why this" quoting a real memory. Claude can only choose a place id from the list, so it can never invent a place. Build: one action, one prompt, a 20-place seed file.

    5The secret tap and the match
"I'm down" and "Only if…" call tap, which writes to taps and runs the matcher in the same transaction. The matcher looks at every subset of tappers (a group of 12 is only 4,096 subsets, which takes milliseconds) and keeps the sets of 2 or more where everyone's "only if" people are in the set and everyone's windows overlap long enough. It picks the biggest set, then the earliest time. Convex transactions are serializable, so two taps landing in the same instant can never reveal twice. If nothing matches, nothing happens and nobody learns anything. Build: about 40 lines of TypeScript, plus a unit test for each rule.

    6"It's on," on every phone in the same frame
When a set matches, the mutation sets status to on and revealAt to now plus 1.5 seconds. Every phone keeps a small clock offset from the server and fires the reveal animation at exactly revealAt, so the faces tumble in together with a haptic. Phones that were waiting get it through their live query; there's no push to wait for. Build: one field, a clock-offset query, the reveal view.

    7Leave in 8, on the lock screen
On reveal, the app starts a Live Activity locally. Its countdown uses Text(timerInterval:), which ticks on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island by itself, so it needs no push updates. "On my way" uploads your MapKit ETA every 30 seconds, and the others see your bubble count down. Build: widget extension, one ActivityAttributes type.

    8Hop in
Once a frolic is on, the frolics query unlocks the names for every free member who wasn't in the match: "Maya and Dev are going. Room for one more?" One tap adds you to members. If someone taps "Can't make it," the Workflow re-runs the match with the hop-in pool and refills the spot. Build: hopIn and bail mutations, one workflow step.

    9Check in by arriving, then a group snap
On reveal, the app registers a 75-meter CLMonitor circle around the spot, and walking in marks you checked in. The photo is the reason to use Frolic over the camera app: a group snap. Anyone taps it, and the mutation sets snapAt 3 seconds out, the same synced-timestamp trick as the reveal. Every matched phone counts down 3, 2, 1 together and fires its camera at the same second. Each photo uploads to Convex file storage, but the others stay hidden until you add yours, so everyone shoots. The shots land as one pressed collage (every angle of the same moment, each tagged with that person's flower, with the place, the date and the time), rendered with ImageRenderer into the garden and a Stories-ready card. In the app the collage is a little 3D diorama: the polaroids sit at different depths and shift as you tilt the phone (Core Motion with rotation3DEffect and per-layer parallax, about an hour). Stretch: on-device depth per photo with Apple's Depth Anything Core ML model, so the foreground lifts off the sky. A memory also goes to Supermemory ("Indian Rock, sunset, 3 of us, Nov 15") to feed tomorrow's "why this." Demo moment: three phones on stage count down together and the collage appears on all of them. Build: geofence, one snapAt field, camera capture at a timestamp, the collage view.

    10Chip in
If the plan costs something, "It's on" shows "Chip in $8" next to directions. WhopCheckout takes Apple Pay and a Whop webhook to a Convex HTTP action marks you paid. Paying up front is the strongest anti-flake there is. Build: checkout sheet, one HTTP action.

    11Memories and the taste card
The scrapbook is every frolic with its photos. The taste card ("Sunset Crew: golden-hour chasers, under $10, night owls") comes from the group's Supermemory profile, rendered to an image with SwiftUI's ImageRenderer at Stories size and handed to the share sheet. Build: one grid, one card view, one share button.

    12The daily drop without push
Without a paid account the server can't wake a phone. Instead, each phone schedules its own local notification for the drop time, with I'm down, Only if and Not today buttons. A button press runs the tap mutation in the background without opening the app. During the demo every phone is open, so live queries make everything instant anyway. With a paid account later, the same buttons move to real push.

    13Useful on day one, with nobody else on it
Instagram grew because the filters were worth using alone, and the network built up behind them. Frolic's version is the plan itself. With no crew yet, Today still shows a real plan picked for you (your taste, the weather, your budget, what's open) and two buttons: "Send to the group chat" and "I'll go solo." Sending posts the seed-packet card into iMessage as a link: "Anyone free for this? Say you're down. It stays secret unless someone else is too." The link opens a small web page served by a Convex HTTP action: the same card, a first-name field and a secret "I'm down" button. Those yeses land in the same taps table and go through the same matcher, so a friend can match with you without installing anything. When it's on, the page flips live through a Convex subscription. Everyone who taps joins your crew automatically, and the app is only needed for the garden, the crew and the daily drop. Demo line: a judge scans a QR code, taps "I'm down" in their phone browser, and our phone blooms. Build: one HTTP route, one web page, a share link and a solo state on Today, about an hour.

  A real app, not a prototype

  Judges have seen a hundred clickable demos. Frolic should be something a friend group could start using tonight. That means:

    Real data end to end. Nothing hard-coded in the demo path. The Convex production deployment, a hand-verified Berkeley set of 60 or more places with real hours and prices, live weather and sunset times, and real accounts on real phones.

    Every state designed. First launch, empty crew, nobody free, loading, offline, a failed plan, a denied permission, an expired link. Each has its own screen and copy, and none is a blank or a spinner that never ends.

    Accessibility built in. Dynamic Type up to the largest size without breaking layouts, VoiceOver labels on every control (flowers announce "Maya, free till 8"), Reduce Motion swaps the bloom for a fade, and AA contrast everywhere.

    Fast. Cold launch to Today in under 1.5 seconds, every tap answered in under 100 ms, and smooth 120 Hz animation on ProMotion phones. Images are cached and sized for the screen.

    Trustworthy. Permissions are asked at the moment they're needed with a one-line reason, a privacy page explains exactly what friends can and can't see, and you can delete your account and data in two taps.

    Real sharing. The web "I'm down" page lives on a real domain with a proper link preview card in iMessage.

  The one hard limit: without the $99 Apple Developer account there's no remote push, no TestFlight, no Sign in with Apple, no App Clip and no universal links, and installs expire after 7 days. Everything above still works, with identity from an invite link and a first name, and live updates while the app is open. If Frolic keeps going after Sunday, that account is the first thing worth paying for.

Cross-app synthesis

1. Why these apps fail to retain

Monetizing the core loop or taking back free features: top low-star driver at Candle (40), Howbout (92), Timeleft (138), 222 (78). Charging both partners, paywalled streak restores, billing after cancel.
Content goes stale: Candle, playus, 222.
Cold start and density: invite walls (Howbout, Locket, 222) and thin stranger supply (Bumble BFF, Timeleft, waitlists).
Flakes and no-shows with no accountability.
Bugs that break streaks or signup.
Ads and guilt notifications inside an intimate product.
AI that feels fake.

2. Unmet needs users ask for

Group mode for the daily ritual (Candle, playus).
A low-effort way to see who is free now, layered on scheduled plans.
Someone else does the planning.
Variety beyond drinking dinners: daytime, outdoor, cheap.
Join without downloading or a phone number.
Fresh, human-feeling content.
Keep and print memories.
Local discovery that is actually local.

3. How our app should differ and win

Friends you already have, never strangers. Four roommates are a complete network on day one.
The 2+ "I'm down" threshold with a short live timer is the anti-flake mechanic.
Quests generated from real context, human-reviewed, rotating formats, seeded by hand-written packs and sponsored spots.
Cheap and daytime by default.
The scrapbook is the retention hook and the pay point.
Adding friends is the first quest; guests RSVP from the web with no install; no contact-invite wall.
Never paywall the core loop or streaks; bill per group; one-tap cancel.
No feed ads; sponsorship lives inside the quest ("free churro if 3 of you show up at X by 4pm").
Couples on-ramp: a group of 2 is the IRL extension of a Candle ritual.

4. Evidence people would pay

Daily shared rituals: Candle ($4.99/wk to $49.99/yr, "10/10 worth paying for"), Locket Gold, Howbout+, playus Pro.
Getting out of the house: Timeleft X ($19.99 to $62.99), 222 stacked fees, Kndrd "$9/month is worth it".
Quest packs already sell at $9.99 to $34.99.
Memories: users ask to print BeReal memories.
Pushback is on price relative to use and charging both people. Per-group pricing plus business-sponsored quests avoids both.

Watch-outs
Share approximate distance only until someone commits.
18+ and friends-only for the college wedge.
Ship stable over clever; lost streaks and SMS failures earn the angriest reviews.
The category is full of tiny clones and two are already delisted. Distribution and density in one campus matter more than features.

  What could kill it

    Cold start. A social app is empty alone. Frolic works with just two friends, and invites go out as a link in the group chat.

    Spontaneity alone doesn't retain. thirdspace stalled near 1K users and was delisted. The daily drop and the scrapbook give a reason to open Frolic even on days nobody's free.

    People without a group. 134 TikTok comments say some version of "I don't have friends." They need it most and are the hardest to serve. Matching them into groups comes later.

    Willingness to pay is unproven for students. Paid packs and sponsored local activities are the plan, and both still need testing.
